How to read this map
  • Each circle is one protected bog. Size and colour show the measure picked below; green is little or none, red is the most. A dark ring marks a bog where NPWS recorded turf cutting in 2021–2022.
  • Exposed peat is bare peat with no vegetation on top: cut turf banks, turf spread to dry, milled or stripped peat, peat bared by fire.
  • Click a bog to open its card: the summer image of each year with exposed peat filled orange, a year slider and play button, and the figures behind them.
  • Hover a circle for its value; filter by legal status or county.
What changed in October 2026

The map now uses detector v5: exposed peat is recognised by its short-wave infrared (SWIR) signature on one clear summer scene per year.

The earlier detector looked for low NDVI on spring scenes. Checked against summer imagery, almost all of what it found turned green: it was winter-dead grass on old cutover, while real exposed peat (NDVI about 0.4) was rejected. On industrial peat fields used as a control it found under 1% of the exposed peat; v5 finds most of it.

Fire also exposes peat. Summers after a fire recorded by NASA FIRMS, and summers with one contiguous patch of 10 ha or more (far larger than any turf plot, so most likely an unrecorded fire), are shown in the bog card but left out of its figures. Figures are research results under review.

The old spring detector stays available under Detector for comparison.

    PlanetScope 3 m view (password)

    v5 maps exposed peat on the clearest Sentinel-2 scene between 15 June and 31 August of each year (rule: SWIR2 above NIR, bright SWIR1, a SWIR ratio that separates fresh burns, and no water). It sees strips of exposed peat about 6 m wide or more; a season's retreat of a cut face (1–2 m) is below that. Exposed peat is evidence of disturbance, not proof of illegal cutting. MSc research prototype; contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data.
