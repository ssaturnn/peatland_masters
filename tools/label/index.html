How to label

Label what covers the yellow 10 m square on the Sentinel-2 date shown. If the square is mixed, choose the cover that fills most of it.

When a PlanetScope 3 m chip is there, read it first: it is the leftmost and largest chip, from within three days of the Sentinel-2 date. Then check the false colour.

Three questions, in order

  1. Is it red or pink in false colour? → 2 Vegetated. Straw-brown heath and grass usually stay pinkish — the false colour decides, not the true colour. Exception — very dry years (2018 was a drought): dead grass can turn beige even in false colour. A large, uniform pale area with no cut banks, over a surface that looks grassy in the high-res map, is dried vegetation, not bare peat — label it Vegetated, or Unsure if you cannot tell.
  2. Is it very dark and smooth — dark blue to black in false colour? → 3 Water: pools, lakes, flooded cuttings, sea.
  3. Neither — look at shape and setting.
    • strips, rectangles or a worked face, usually at the bog margin; turf banks in the high-res map → 1 Bare peat. Dark brown when fresh or wet, pale tan when dry.
    • a large irregular charcoal-dark patch across the bog surface, no bank geometry → 4 Burn scar.
    • road, track, building, rock, sand, tidal mud → 5 Other.

Cloud or shadow on the square, or truly half-and-half → 0 Unsure. Keep it rare: unsure points are left out of the metrics.

The high-resolution map shows what kind of place this is — banks, pools, tracks — but it may date from another year. The Sentinel-2 chips decide the state on the date.

PlanetScope 3 m decides where it exists (140 of the 180 points; its acquisition date is in the caption). It shows the same state three times sharper than Sentinel-2, so use it for bare peat versus dry grass: smooth surfaces with straight edges are cut peat, tussocky texture is vegetation. Where no PlanetScope chip appears, the Sentinel-2 close-ups decide as before, and the evidence box records which of the two you used.

Classes

1 · Bare peat
Exposed peat: fresh cut faces, spread or stacked turf, milled or stripped surfaces. Dark brown when fresh or wet, pale tan to beige once dry. In false colour brown, beige or grey — never red.
2 · Vegetated
Any plant cover, living or dead: heath, grass, rush, moss, scrub, forest, including winter-brown or dry vegetation. Usually red to pink in false colour; very dry dead grass can look beige, and then texture, extent and the absence of cut banks decide.
3 · Water
Lakes, pools, flooded cuttings, sea, drains wide enough to fill the square. Dark blue-green to black in true colour, very dark blue in false colour, smooth.
4 · Burn scar
Recently burnt vegetation: a large irregular patch, charcoal-black to very dark brown, without bank geometry. Very dark in false colour.
5 · Other
Roads, tracks, buildings, rock, sand, gravel, tidal mud, bare mineral soil.
0 · Unsure
Cloud, cloud shadow, or genuinely undecidable.
